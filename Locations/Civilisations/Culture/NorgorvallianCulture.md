# Norgorvallian culture
Norgorvallian culture has preserved its traditional virtues and ways. Some may even say that people of this culture are living in the past.

## Virtues
Because of the glorified opinion of nature, some of the most noble virtues in Norgorvallian culture are resourcefulness and hardiness. Even though they stubbornly hold onto traditional beliefs of masculinity and femininity, people still have freedom in many things.

Despite having a culture of hospitality, many settlements of Norgorvallian culture do not have an inn or tavern because they are focused on residence and farming.

## Clothing
The most common textile is wool. Men often wear knee-length tunics, trousers, leg wraps, and shoes; women often wear dresses ranging from calf- to ankle-length.

In cold weather, they have a simple cloak stitched together with square pieces, sometimes with a hood. It usually reaches down to their knees.

Some men may wear their tunics with a riding split, especially if their clothing is longer.

Amongst the nobility, clothing may have elaborate embroidery near the collar and sleeves, and cloaks may also be made of hide.

## Architecture
Village buildings are fully wooden longhouses with sod roofing. They often have just one floor and have a small enclosure for farm animals and orchards. Lighting usually comes from a long central hearth in the middle of the longhouse, with smoke expelled from a small opening in the ceiling. Outside lighting is often provided by standing torches, and sometimes, lanterns.

City buildings are of course more refined, and are more like houses than halls. Although walls are often made of wood and are covered with clay, some buildings' first-floor walls are instead made of stone. Roofs are made using simple clay tiles. Lighting usually comes from lanterns (and sometimes a square hearth), but richer households/buildings may have braziers.
